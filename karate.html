Lineage & Heritage

The Funakoshi Shotokan Tradition

Shotokan Karate was founded by Master Gichin Funakoshi (1868–1957) in Okinawa and introduced to mainland Japan in 1922. The term "Shoto" was Funakoshi's pen name meaning "pine waves", and "Kan" means hall or dojo.

Sunninghill Shotokan Karate was founded in 2015 to preserve and transmit this authentic Funakoshi style. Our dojo emphasizes deep, stable stances, explosive linear hip rotation, precise biomechanics, and above all, the moral development of each student.

The Tiger Symbol (Tora no Maki):

The tiger depicted in our official emblem is the traditional symbol of Shotokan Karate, drawn by Japanese artist Hoan Kosugi for Master Funakoshi’s foundational book. It represents fierce courage, intense focus, alertness, and noble restraint.

Discipline · Character · Excellence

Pillar 01

Kihon (Fundamentals)

The foundation of all karate technique. Practicing punches (Tsuki), strikes (Uchi), kicks (Geri), and blocks (Uke) from powerful stances (Zenkutsu, Kokutsu, Kiba Dachi) to develop muscle memory, explosive power, and perfect balance.

Pillar 02

Kata (Forms)

Pre-arranged sequences of defensive and offensive movements against imaginary opponents. Kata teaches spatial awareness, rhythm, breath control, and the practical application (Bunkai) of self-defense techniques.

Pillar 03

Kumite (Sparring)

The practical application of karate against a partner. Taught progressively—from structured five-step sparring (Gohon Kumite) and semi-free sparring (Jiyu Ippon Kumite) to free sparring (Jiyu Kumite) with strict control and mutual respect.

Journey to Black Belt

Belt Grading & Syllabus

Every belt represents months of dedicated training, personal growth, and mastery of increasingly demanding techniques.

White Belt (9th Kyu)
Beginner Level

Focus on dojo etiquette, stances (Zenkutsu-dachi), basic blocks (Gedan-barai, Age-uke), straight punch (Oi-zuki), and front kick (Mae-geri). Introduction to Taikyoku Shodan.

Yellow & Orange Belts (8th & 7th Kyu)
Novice Level

Developing combinations, Kokutsu-dachi (back stance), Soto-uke, Uchi-uke, and Yoko-geri (side kick). Mastery of Kata Heian Shodan and Heian Nidan.

Green & Blue Belts (6th & 5th Kyu)
Intermediate Level

Kiba-dachi (horse stance), Mawashi-geri (roundhouse kick), Empi-uchi (elbow strike), and introductory semi-free sparring (Jiyu Ippon Kumite). Mastery of Heian Sandan and Heian Yondan.

Purple & Red Belts (4th & 3rd Kyu)
Advanced Intermediate

Refining hip rotation, rapid transitions, counter-attacks, and dynamic sparring timing. Mastery of Heian Godan and Tekki Shodan.

Brown Belt (2nd & 1st Kyu)
Senior Student Level

Intense preparation for Dan grading. Advanced Katas: Bassai Dai, Kanku Dai, Jion, and Enpi. High-level free sparring and student mentoring.

Black Belt (Shodan & Above)
Mastery of the Fundamentals

The true beginning of the karate journey. Embodying the Dojo Kun, technical excellence, deep understanding of Bunkai, Kobudo, and lifelong dedication.

Guiding Principles

The Five Maxims of the Dojo Kun

Recited at the conclusion of every karate class, the Dojo Kun reminds each karateka that training extends beyond physical movements into everyday living.

01
Seek perfection of character
Hitotsu, Jinkaku Kansei Ni Tsutomeru Koto

Karate begins with courtesy and ends with respect. Build integrity, honesty, and humility.

02
Be faithful and sincere
Hitotsu, Makoto No Michi Wo Mamoru Koto

Dedication to truth, loyalty to your instructors, and reliability toward your fellow students.

03
Endeavor and persevere
Hitotsu, Doryoku No Seishin Wo Yashinau Koto

Cultivate tenacity and perseverance. Genuine martial mastery requires sustained daily effort.

04
Respect others and maintain etiquette
Hitotsu, Reigi Wo Omonzuru Koto

Etiquette inside the dojo is sacred. Treat elders, instructors, juniors, and opponents with dignity.

05
Refrain from violent behavior & control temper
Hitotsu, Kekki No Yu Wo Imashimuru Koto

True courage is keeping calm under pressure. A true karateka uses skill only when left with no other choice.
